\title{Mixing Matters: Isolating the Architectural Origin of Position Bias

\begin{abstract}
Language models attend unevenly to evidence depending on where it sits in
the context, the ``lost-in-the-middle'' effect. We ask a controlled
question: at matched pretraining data, scale, tokenizer, and context, does
changing the sequence-mixing architecture from dense causal attention to a
Mamba state-space model (SSM) change the accuracy-versus-position curve? We
build a deterministic ten-position measurement on the Lost-in-the-Middle
multi-document QA task, define a \emph{primacy} and a \emph{recency} edge,
and test both with a paired bootstrap over questions (10{,}000 resamples,
Holm-corrected). Across a matched 2.8B triple we find a primacy arm in the
transformer ($+0.052$, Holm $p<10^{-4}$) that is absent in both Mamba
variants ($-0.001$ and $-0.018$); the recency arm is shared. A matched 8B
isolation confirms the architectural origin: adding $\sim$7\% attention
layers to an otherwise identical pure Mamba-2 turns a null primacy arm
($+0.008$, $p=0.36$) into a significant one ($+0.027$, $p=0.007$). The
primacy gap emerges with scale ($\sim$1B parameters), survives a
Pile$\rightarrow$SlimPajama corpus swap (null effect, $p=0.57$), and
reappears on a synthetic needle task and across three production 8B systems.
Mechanistically, late-layer attention-sink mass tracks the primacy arm; a
linear probe recovers gold position \emph{more} strongly from Mamba than from
the transformer ($0.65$ vs $0.60$) while Mamba shows no primacy, indicating
utilisation rather than storage; and a training-free prompt intervention
(placing the query before the documents) lifts Mamba's primacy from null to
$+0.103$. The effect is a property of how attention uses position, not of
data, task, or the model's ability to locate evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Transformers read multi-document contexts unevenly: accuracy is highest when
the answer-bearing document sits at the start or end of the context and
lowest in the middle \citep{liu2024lost}. State-space models such as Mamba
\citep{gu2023mamba,dao2024mamba2} mix sequence information through a
fixed-size recurrent state rather than all-pairs attention, and it is not
obvious they should share the same position profile. Prior comparisons
confound architecture with pretraining corpus, tokenizer, scale, and
capability, so a raw curve difference cannot be attributed to the sequence
mixer.

We isolate that variable. Our contribution is threefold. (i) A
\emph{trustworthy measurement}: a fully deterministic ten-position sweep with
floor/ceiling anchors, vendored scoring, pinned runtimes, and negative,
distractor-order, and positive controls, analysed with a question-level
paired bootstrap. (ii) A \emph{controlled architecture contrast}: at matched
2.8B scale on the Pile the transformer has a primacy arm the two Mamba
models lack, and a matched 8B experiment that changes \emph{only} whether
$\sim$7\% attention layers are present reproduces the effect, isolating
attention as the cause. (iii) A \emph{mechanism}: attention-sink mass tracks
primacy, a position probe shows Mamba stores location it does not use, and a
training-free prompt reordering recovers the arm in Mamba. Corpus and task
change the level of the curve, not its shape.

\section{Measurement and statistics}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Task and anchors.} We use the Lost-in-the-Middle 10-document QA
set \citep{liu2024lost}, 2{,}655 released questions, each with one gold
document and nine distractors. For every question we build ten prompts,
placing the gold document at each position $0$--$9$ with the distractor set
and prompt length held fixed, so position is the only quantity that moves.
Per question we also run a closed-book \emph{floor} (no gold document,
measuring guessing and memorised knowledge) and an oracle \emph{ceiling}
(gold document alone, measuring retrieval-perfect answerability).

\paragraph{Edges.} With mean accuracy at positions $\{0,1\}$, $\{4,5\}$,
$\{8,9\}$ denoted $a_{\text{start}},a_{\text{mid}},a_{\text{end}}$, we define
$\textsc{primacy}=a_{\text{start}}-a_{\text{mid}}$ and
$\textsc{recency}=a_{\text{end}}-a_{\text{mid}}$. A positive primacy edge is
the start-of-context advantage; a positive recency edge is the
end-of-context advantage.

\paragraph{Statistics.} The question is the unit of analysis: a bootstrap
resample draws whole questions with replacement, carrying all ten positions
together, and every contrast is recomputed on each of 10{,}000 resamples to
form percentile confidence intervals. When two models are compared the same
resample of question ids is reused for both, making the interaction paired.
We Holm-correct across the primacy and recency tests. All numbers below are
on a fixed 800-question exploratory split; a 1{,}855-question confirmatory
split is held out and unspent.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and controls.} Scoring vendors
\texttt{best\_subspan\_em} and the prompt builders from
\citet{liu2024lost} at a pinned commit; two secondary scores (normalized
exact match, first-line extraction) are recorded alongside. Generation is
greedy at temperature $0$, 32 new tokens, BF16, fixed seed, with pinned
PyTorch/Transformers/\texttt{mamba-ssm}/\texttt{causal-conv1d} versions and
pinned model revisions; every Mamba run uses the one CUDA-kernel execution
path (the runner refuses to fall back). Raw prompts and generations are never
overwritten and are preserved even when scoring fails. Three controls
validate the instrument: a negative control (replace gold with a fake
document; position effect must vanish), a distractor-order control (shuffle
distractors at fixed gold position; accuracy must be stable), and the
released key-value retrieval positive control.

\section{The architecture contrast}

\paragraph{Matched 2.8B triple (Pile).} We compare
\texttt{pythia-2.8b} (dense attention, partial RoPE, 32 layers) with
\texttt{mamba-2.8b} and \texttt{mamba2-2.7b} (SSMs, no explicit positional
encoding, $\sim$64 layers), all pretrained on the Pile with related
tokenizers and no instruction alignment. Table~\ref{tab:phase2} and
Figure~\ref{fig:phase2} show the result: the transformer has a clear primacy
arm; both SSMs do not. The paired interaction excludes zero for both Mamba
models (Pythia$-$Mamba-1 $=-0.053$; Pythia$-$Mamba-2 $=-0.070$; both Holm
$p<10^{-4}$), and the two Mamba models do not differ. The recency arm is
present and positive for all three.

\paragraph{Matched 8B attention isolation.} The 2.8B contrast still differs
in depth and positional encoding. To isolate attention alone we use two
NVIDIA 8B checkpoints trained on identical data with the same tokenizer,
scale, depth, and positional-encoding setup, differing only in that the
hybrid replaces $\sim$7\% of its Mamba-2 blocks with attention. Both load
through one Megatron execution path. Adding attention turns a null primacy
arm into a significant one:

The pure model has no primacy arm; adding attention creates one and
simultaneously shrinks recency (hybrid$-$pure recency
$=-0.028$, $p=0.09$). This is the cleanest single-variable evidence that
attention layers, not depth or positional encoding, produce the primacy arm
(Figure~\ref{fig:phase3}).

\section{Robustness of the contrast}

\paragraph{Scale (Phase 4).} Sweeping five matched Pythia--Mamba size pairs
from 130M to 2.8B, the Pythia$-$Mamba primacy difference is indistinguishable
from zero at the two smallest pairs ($-0.001$, $+0.003$), then appears at
790M--1B ($+0.062$) and holds through 2.8B ($+0.053$): the gap
\emph{grows} with scale (endpoint change $+0.054$) and stabilises once both
families can answer at all (Figure~\ref{fig:phase4}). Recency differences
stay near zero once both are capable.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.6\linewidth]{figures/phase4-scale-gap.png}
\caption{Pythia$-$Mamba primacy (and recency) edge difference across five
matched size pairs. The primacy gap is null below $\sim$1B and emerges and
holds above it.}
\label{fig:phase4}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Training corpus (Phase 5).} Holding the Mamba-2.8B architecture
fixed and swapping the Pile for SlimPajama moves neither edge: primacy effect
$-0.007$ ($p=0.57$), recency effect $-0.016$ ($p=0.50$), both intervals
spanning zero. The corpus lifts the whole curve (floor $0.11\rightarrow0.27$)
while the ceiling barely moves ($0.615\rightarrow0.625$): more memorised
answers, not a change in how position is used. Swapping the architecture
moved primacy; swapping the corpus did not.

\paragraph{Task (Phase 6).} On the RULER \texttt{niah\_single\_1} synthetic
needle task at 2K tokens the transformer primacy arm reappears
($+0.12$, $p<10^{-4}$), larger than on QA; the Mamba models saturate at
accuracy $1.0$ at every needle depth, so their zero edges are task
saturation, not position invariance. The shape is not a QA-format artifact.

\paragraph{Production systems (Phase 8).} As a descriptive check we run three
complete 8B systems that differ on every axis at once
(\texttt{Llama-3.1-8B}, \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B}, \texttt{Nemotron-H-8B}). All
three show a Holm-significant primacy arm ($+0.076$, $+0.041$, $+0.067$): the
phenomenon survives replacing the entire training pipeline. The recency arm
is cleanest on Nemotron-H, the one system containing SSM blocks, echoing the
matched result that SSM mixing favours recency.

\section{Mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Six candidate causes were named a priori; the controlled experiments assign
each a verdict.

\paragraph{Not depth.} Within the Pythia family the one contrast where depth
and width move oppositely is Pythia-410M (24 layers) versus Pythia-1B (16
layers): the deeper, narrower model has the \emph{smaller} primacy arm
($+0.013$, n.s.\ vs $+0.052$). Depth alone does not manufacture the arm.

\paragraph{Not a measurement artifact.} Edge signs and Holm-significance
survive both secondary scorers and every prompt-length tertile. The
architecture contrast is template-invariant (Mamba primacy null under three
instruction templates; Pythia positive under two). The transformer's primacy
\emph{magnitude} is template-sensitive---an instructional template that
prepends a long header collapses Pythia primacy from $+0.070$ to $+0.005$
while leaving recency intact---which is itself consistent with the sink
account below, since the header displaces the sink-bearing prompt head.

\paragraph{Attention sinks track primacy.} Measuring token-0 attention share
per layer across the Pythia family, the model with a null primacy arm
(160M) has almost no sink (final-layer $0.003$) while models with a primacy
arm carry a substantial one, peaking at $0.455$ in the final layer of
Pythia-2.8B (Figure~\ref{fig:sink}). Pythia-410M is diagnostic: its
mid-network sink is high but collapses by the output layer ($0.034$) and its
primacy is not significant---it is \emph{late-layer} sink mass, surviving to
the output, that couples to the arm.

\paragraph{Utilisation, not storage.} A balanced linear probe for
gold-at-edge versus gold-at-middle, trained on frozen mid-depth hidden states
with a shuffled-label control at chance, recovers position from
\emph{both} architectures---and \emph{more} strongly from Mamba
($0.648$) than from Pythia ($0.603$)---yet Mamba has no primacy arm. Both
architectures locate the gold document; only the transformer converts that
location into an edge-accuracy advantage. Storage is not the bottleneck.

\paragraph{Fixed-state compression and a training-free intervention.} Moving
the query before the documents lets Mamba's recurrent state filter documents
as it compresses them. This training-free reordering lifts Mamba's primacy
from a null baseline to $+0.103$ ($p<10^{-4}$)---the largest Mamba primacy
edge we observe---and, when the query is placed only before, halves its
recency ($0.070\rightarrow0.010$). For the transformer the same bookend
placement doubles primacy (extra query tokens in the sink-bearing head) and
padding tokens between documents and question halve it, both matching the
sink account.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

The results converge on one account. Dense causal attention learns a
late-layer attention sink at the prompt head that biases the answer toward
early context, producing a primacy arm; the arm requires enough capability
(around 1B parameters) and is created, at matched data and depth, by adding
attention layers. A Mamba SSM has no sink and no primacy arm even though its
recurrent state encodes gold position at least as well as the transformer's;
its state compression instead yields a recency arm, and exposing the query
early converts that same state into a primacy arm on demand. Corpus and task
change the level of the curve, not its shape.

Limitations. All numbers are on the exploratory split; the confirmatory
split remains frozen. The 2.8B and scale contrasts leave depth and
positional encoding partly confounded, which is why the matched 8B
experiment is load-bearing. The sink-blocking ablation on the hybrid was a
null intervention because the vendored custom attention does not expose or
honour an attention-mask hook, and the hybrid's own sink mass could not be
read for the same reason; a direct forward patch is left as future work.
Probe accuracies are modest ($0.60$--$0.65$); the claim is the direction
(Mamba encodes position at least as well while showing no arm), not the
absolute decodability.

\section{Conclusion}

Under matched pretraining data and token count, changing the sequence-mixing
architecture changes the accuracy-versus-position curve: dense attention adds
a primacy arm that a Mamba SSM lacks, isolated by a matched 8B experiment
that varies only the presence of attention layers. The arm is scale-emergent,
corpus- and task-robust, and driven by late-layer attention-sink mass rather
than by data, depth, or the model's ability to locate evidence. A
training-free query-first reordering recovers the arm in Mamba, showing the
difference is one of utilisation, not storage.
