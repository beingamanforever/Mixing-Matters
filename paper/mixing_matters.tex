\begin{abstract}
Language models weigh evidence unevenly by its position in the context, the
lost-in-the-middle effect. We ask a controlled question: at matched
pretraining data, scale, tokenizer, and context, does changing the
sequence mixer from dense causal attention to a Mamba state-space model
change the accuracy-versus-position curve? We build a deterministic
ten-position measurement on multi-document QA, define a primacy and a
recency edge, and test both with a paired bootstrap over questions (10{,}000
resamples, Holm-corrected). On a matched 2.8B triple the transformer has a
primacy arm ($+0.052$, Holm $p<10^{-4}$) that both Mamba variants lack
($-0.001$, $-0.018$); recency is shared. A matched 8B experiment isolates
the cause: adding about 7 percent attention layers to an otherwise identical
pure Mamba-2 turns a null primacy arm ($+0.008$, $p=0.36$) into a
significant one ($+0.027$, $p=0.007$). The gap emerges with scale, survives
a corpus swap ($p=0.57$), reappears on a synthetic needle task, and holds
across three production 8B systems. Late-layer attention-sink mass tracks
the primacy arm; a linear probe recovers gold position more strongly from
Mamba than from the transformer ($0.65$ vs $0.60$) while Mamba shows no
primacy, indicating utilisation rather than storage; and a training-free
prompt reordering lifts Mamba primacy from null to $+0.103$. The primacy arm
is a property of how attention uses position, not of data, task, or the
ability to locate evidence.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}


Transformers read multi-document contexts unevenly: accuracy peaks when the
answer-bearing document sits at the start or end and dips in the middle
\citep{liu2024lost}. State-space models such as Mamba
\citep{gu2023mamba,dao2024mamba2} mix sequence information through a
fixed-size recurrent state rather than all-pairs attention, and need not
share the same position profile. Existing comparisons confound architecture
with pretraining corpus, tokenizer, scale, and capability, so a raw curve
difference cannot be pinned on the sequence mixer.


We isolate that variable. Our contributions are: (i) a trustworthy
measurement, a fully deterministic ten-position sweep with floor and ceiling
anchors, vendored scoring, pinned runtimes, and negative, distractor-order,
and positive controls, analysed with a question-level paired bootstrap; (ii)
a controlled architecture contrast, where at matched 2.8B scale the
transformer has a primacy arm the Mamba models lack, and a matched 8B
experiment that changes only whether about 7 percent attention layers are
present reproduces it; and (iii) a mechanism, where attention-sink mass
tracks primacy, a position probe shows Mamba stores location it does not use,
and a training-free prompt reordering recovers the arm in Mamba. Corpus and
task change the level of the curve, not its shape.


\section{Measurement and statistics}
\label{sec:method}


\paragraph{Task and anchors.}
We use the Lost-in-the-Middle 10-document QA set \citep{liu2024lost}: 2{,}655
released questions, each with one gold document and nine distractors. Per
question we build ten prompts, placing the gold document at each position $0$
to $9$ with the distractor set and prompt length fixed, so position is the
only quantity that moves. We also run a closed-book floor (no gold document,
measuring guessing and memorised knowledge) and an oracle ceiling (gold
document alone, measuring retrieval-perfect answerability).


\paragraph{Edges.}
With mean accuracy at positions $\{0,1\}$, $\{4,5\}$, $\{8,9\}$ written
$a_{\text{start}}$, $a_{\text{mid}}$, $a_{\text{end}}$, we define
$\textsc{primacy} = a_{\text{start}} - a_{\text{mid}}$ and
$\textsc{recency} = a_{\text{end}} - a_{\text{mid}}$. A positive primacy edge
is a start-of-context advantage; a positive recency edge is an
end-of-context advantage.


\paragraph{Statistics.}
The question is the unit of analysis: a bootstrap resample draws whole
questions with replacement, carrying all ten positions together, and each
contrast is recomputed on 10{,}000 resamples to form percentile intervals.
Comparing two models reuses the same resample for both, making the
interaction paired. We Holm-correct across the primacy and recency tests.
All numbers below are on a fixed 800-question exploratory split; a
1{,}855-question confirmatory split is held out and unspent.


\paragraph{Reproducibility and controls.}
Scoring vendors \texttt{best\_subspan\_em} and the prompt builders from
\citet{liu2024lost} at a pinned commit; two secondary scores (normalized
exact match, first-line extraction) are recorded alongside. Generation is
greedy at temperature $0$, 32 new tokens, BF16, fixed seed, with pinned
library and model revisions; every Mamba run uses one CUDA-kernel execution
path and the runner refuses to fall back. Raw prompts and generations are
never overwritten and are kept even when scoring fails. Three controls
validate the instrument: a negative control (replace gold with a fake
document, the position effect must vanish), a distractor-order control
(shuffle distractors at fixed gold position, accuracy must be stable), and
the released key-value retrieval positive control, which passes for the
transformer and Mamba-2 and fails for Mamba-1 (its 16-slot state cannot hold
30 pairs), a known-effect check on the pipeline.


\section{The architecture contrast}
\label{sec:contrast}


\paragraph{Matched 2.8B triple.}
We compare \texttt{pythia-2.8b} (dense attention, partial rotary encoding, 32
layers) \citep{biderman2023pythia} with \texttt{mamba-2.8b} and
\texttt{mamba2-2.7b} (state-space models, no explicit positional encoding,
about 64 layers), all pretrained on the Pile \citep{gao2020pile} with related
tokenizers and no instruction alignment. Table~\ref{tab:phase2} and
Figure~\ref{fig:phase2} show the result: the transformer has a clear primacy
arm, both state-space models do not. The paired interaction excludes zero for
both Mamba models (Pythia minus Mamba-1 $= -0.053$; Pythia minus Mamba-2
$= -0.070$; both Holm $p<10^{-4}$), and the two Mamba models do not differ.
Recency is present and positive for all three.

\paragraph{Matched 8B attention isolation.}
The 2.8B contrast still differs in depth and positional encoding. To isolate
attention alone we use two NVIDIA 8B checkpoints
\citep{waleffe2024empirical} trained on identical data with the same
tokenizer, scale, depth, and positional-encoding setup, differing only in
that the hybrid replaces about 7 percent of its Mamba-2 blocks with
attention. Both load through one execution path. Adding attention turns a
null primacy arm into a significant one (Table~\ref{tab:phase3},
Figure~\ref{fig:phase3}): the pure model has no primacy arm ($+0.008$,
$p=0.36$), the hybrid does ($+0.027$, $p=0.007$), and recency shrinks in step
(hybrid minus pure recency $=-0.028$, $p=0.09$). This is the cleanest
single-variable evidence that attention layers, not depth or positional
encoding, produce the primacy arm.

\section{Robustness of the contrast}
\label{sec:robust}


\paragraph{Scale.}
Sweeping five matched Pythia and Mamba size pairs from 130M to 2.8B, the
Pythia minus Mamba primacy difference is indistinguishable from zero at the
two smallest pairs ($-0.001$, $+0.003$), then appears at 790M--1B ($+0.062$)
and holds through 2.8B ($+0.053$): the gap grows with scale (endpoint change
$+0.054$) and stabilises once both families can answer (Figure~\ref{fig:phase4}).


\paragraph{Training corpus.}
Holding the Mamba-2.8B architecture fixed and swapping the Pile for
SlimPajama \citep{soboleva2023slimpajama} moves neither edge: primacy effect
$-0.007$ ($p=0.57$), recency effect $-0.016$ ($p=0.50$), both intervals
spanning zero. The corpus lifts the whole curve (floor $0.11$ to $0.27$)
while the ceiling barely moves ($0.615$ to $0.625$): more memorised answers,
not a change in how position is used. Swapping the architecture moved
primacy; swapping the corpus did not.


\paragraph{Task.}
On the RULER \texttt{niah\_single\_1} synthetic needle task
\citep{hsieh2024ruler} at 2K tokens the transformer primacy arm reappears
($+0.12$, $p<10^{-4}$), larger than on QA; the Mamba models saturate at
accuracy $1.0$ at every needle depth, so their zero edges are task
saturation, not position invariance. The shape is not a QA-format artifact.


\paragraph{Production systems.}
As a descriptive check we run three complete 8B systems that differ on every
axis at once: \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B} \citep{dubey2024llama},
\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B} \citep{yang2024qwen2}, and \texttt{Nemotron-H-8B}
\citep{nvidia2025nemotronh}. All three show a Holm-significant primacy arm
($+0.076$, $+0.041$, $+0.067$): the phenomenon survives replacing the entire
training pipeline. Recency is cleanest on Nemotron-H, the one system with
state-space blocks, echoing the matched result that state-space mixing favours
recency.

\section{Mechanism}
\label{sec:mechanism}


Six candidate causes were named a priori; the controlled experiments assign
each a verdict (Table~\ref{tab:verdicts}).


\paragraph{Not depth.}
Within the Pythia family the one contrast where depth and width move
oppositely is Pythia-410M (24 layers) versus Pythia-1B (16 layers): the
deeper, narrower model has the smaller primacy arm ($+0.013$, not significant,
versus $+0.052$). Depth alone does not manufacture the arm.


\paragraph{Not a measurement artifact.}
Edge signs and Holm-significance survive both secondary scorers and every
prompt-length tertile. The architecture contrast is template-invariant (Mamba
primacy null under three instruction templates; Pythia positive under two).
The transformer's primacy magnitude is template-sensitive, since an
instructional template that prepends a long header collapses Pythia primacy
from $+0.070$ to $+0.005$ while leaving recency intact, which is itself
consistent with the sink account below because the header displaces the
sink-bearing prompt head.


\paragraph{Attention sinks track primacy.}
Measuring token-0 attention share per layer across the Pythia family
\citep{xiao2024efficient}, the model with a null primacy arm (160M) has almost
no sink (final-layer $0.003$), while models with a primacy arm carry a
substantial one, peaking at $0.455$ in the final layer of Pythia-2.8B
(Figure~\ref{fig:sink}). Pythia-410M is diagnostic: its mid-network sink is
high but collapses by the output layer ($0.034$) and its primacy is not
significant. It is late-layer sink mass, surviving to the output, that
couples to the arm.


\paragraph{Utilisation, not storage.}
A balanced linear probe for gold-at-edge versus gold-at-middle, trained on
frozen mid-depth hidden states with a shuffled-label control at chance,
recovers position from both architectures, and more strongly from Mamba
($0.648$) than from Pythia ($0.603$), yet Mamba has no primacy arm. Both
architectures locate the gold document; only the transformer converts that
location into an edge-accuracy advantage. Storage is not the bottleneck.


\paragraph{Fixed-state compression and a training-free intervention.}
Moving the query before the documents lets Mamba's recurrent state filter
documents as it compresses them. This training-free reordering lifts Mamba
primacy from a null baseline to $+0.103$ ($p<10^{-4}$), the largest Mamba
primacy edge we observe, and, when the query is placed only before, halves
its recency ($0.070$ to $0.010$). For the transformer the same placement
doubles primacy (extra query tokens in the sink-bearing head) and padding
tokens between documents and question halve it, both matching the sink
account.

\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}


The results converge on one account. Dense causal attention learns a
late-layer attention sink at the prompt head that biases the answer toward
early context, producing a primacy arm; the arm needs enough capability
(around 1B parameters) and is created, at matched data and depth, by adding
attention layers. A Mamba state-space model has no sink and no primacy arm
even though its recurrent state encodes gold position at least as well as the
transformer's; its state compression instead yields a recency arm, and
exposing the query early converts that same state into a primacy arm on
demand. Corpus and task change the level of the curve, not its shape.


Limitations. All numbers are on the exploratory split; the confirmatory split
remains frozen. The 2.8B and scale contrasts leave depth and positional
encoding partly confounded, which is why the matched 8B experiment is
load-bearing. A sink-blocking ablation on the hybrid was a null intervention
because the vendored custom attention does not expose or honour an
attention-mask hook, and the hybrid's own sink mass could not be read for the
same reason; a direct forward patch is left as future work. Probe accuracies
are modest ($0.60$ to $0.65$); the claim is the direction, that Mamba encodes
position at least as well while showing no arm, not the absolute
decodability.


\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}


Under matched pretraining data and token count, changing the sequence mixer
changes the accuracy-versus-position curve: dense attention adds a primacy arm
that a Mamba state-space model lacks, isolated by a matched 8B experiment
that varies only the presence of attention layers. The arm is scale-emergent,
corpus- and task-robust, and driven by late-layer attention-sink mass rather
than by data, depth, or the ability to locate evidence. A training-free
query-first reordering recovers the arm in Mamba, showing the difference is
one of utilisation, not storage.
